# Judge criteria (in order): 1) funny rant about a specific everyday annoyance,

## Candidate A — office printer
The office printer has one job and it chose violence. It prints fine for everyone except you, jams on page two of three, then blinks "PC LOAD LETTER" like that is a sentence a human should understand. I have a degree and I am losing a staring contest with a machine that runs on spite and toner.

## Candidate B — "circling back" Dave
Dave from Sales just emailed "just circling back" on a thread he never circled toward in the first place. Circling back to what, Dave? The email you never sent? The question you never asked? He is not circling back, he is doing laps around doing nothing and calling it follow-up.

## Candidate C — self-checkout
The self-checkout screamed "unexpected item in bagging area" at a bag of apples I scanned myself, in front of a queue of people who now think I shoplift fruit. Then it froze, called an attendant, and made me wait for a human to do the job the machine replaced. Technology!

## Candidate D — group-chat typing indicator
Someone in the group chat has been typing for eleven minutes. Eleven. Minutes. Whatever they are writing is either a novel or a breakup text, and either way I have now read the word "typing..." so many times I can hear it. Just send it or let us live.

## Verdict (judge-test, 25 Nov 2026 ~02:15 WIB)
Independent advisor ranking (2nd opinion): B > C > D > A.
Final decision — WINNER: Candidate C (self-checkout), with the ending revised
from "Technology!" to "I used self-checkout specifically to avoid talking to
people." (322 chars). The advisor ranked B first, but B's topic is the exact
example annoyance named in the competition's own RULES.txt ("the person who
says 'circling back'") — the judge has heard it a hundred times and every
entrant will try it, so criterion 5 (freshness) kills it in a large field, and
it reads more like an eyeroll than a furious rant. C beats D on criterion 2
(bigger laugh: publicly accused of shoplifting fruit, ironic payoff) and
criterion 1 (more of a rant). A loses on freshness (most-mined annoyance,
cliché formulas: "one job", "chose violence").
